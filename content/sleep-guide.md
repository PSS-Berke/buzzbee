## The 6 essentials

Every great mattress, regardless of price or brand, gets these six right. Most bad mattresses fail on one or two. Use this list as a checklist when you shop.

### 1. Support

**What it does:** Keeps your spine in a neutral line whether you sleep on your side, back, or stomach.

**How to test:** Lie in your normal sleep position for at least five minutes. Have a friend or partner photograph your spine from the side. It should look the same shape it does when you stand — not bowed, not arched.

**Common failure:** Cheap foam beds that "hug" you for a week, then sag in the middle. The mattress is supporting itself, not you.

### 2. Feel

**What it does:** The first impression — soft, plush, firm, hard. The thing you notice in the first thirty seconds.

**How to test:** Don't trust thirty seconds. The honeymoon feel changes once you've slept on it for two weeks. This is why every reputable mattress company offers a home trial.

**Common failure:** Buying for the showroom feel. The bed you love at 2 p.m. on a Tuesday in a store may not be the bed you love at 3 a.m. on a Wednesday at home.

### 3. Temperature

**What it does:** Wicks heat away from your body so you don't wake up sweating.

**How to test:** Place your hand on the mattress for thirty seconds, then lift it. A heat-trapping mattress will feel sweaty against your palm. A breathable one will feel neutral.

**Common failure:** Pure memory foam without ventilation. Memory foam gets a bad reputation it doesn't fully deserve — the issue is foam without airflow, not foam itself.

### 4. Stability

**What it does:** Stops the bed from rocking when one person turns over.

**How to test:** Lie on one side. Have a partner sit on the other side and shift their weight. Can you feel it across the bed? You shouldn't, in a great mattress.

**Common failure:** Old-style innersprings where every coil is connected. One person's roll becomes the other person's earthquake.

### 5. Motion isolation

**What it does:** A close cousin of stability — specifically, when one person gets in or out of bed, the other shouldn't bounce.

**How to test:** Place a glass of water in the middle of the bed. Have a partner sit down at the foot. Did the water spill?

**Common failure:** Bargain hybrids that compromise on the coil quality. Individually wrapped coils ("pocket coils") are the difference here.

### 6. Materials

**What it does:** Determines the lifespan of the mattress, your exposure to chemical off-gassing, and how the bed feels against your skin if you sleep on it without sheets (which, please don't).

**How to test:** Read the label. Ask the brand to tell you exactly what's in each layer, by name. If they can't, that's an answer.

**Common failure:** Polyfoam that breaks down in 18 months. "Premium memory foam" is not a specification.

## How to read a mattress spec sheet

The mattress industry has invented a small vocabulary of marketing words that don't actually describe a product. Here's the translation key.

### Words that mean something

- **Pocket coils / individually wrapped coils.** Each spring is in its own fabric pocket, so they move independently. Better motion isolation, better support contour.
- **Latex.** Either natural (from rubber trees) or synthetic. Natural latex is durable, breathable, and expensive. Either is better than low-density polyfoam.
- **GOTS / GOLS / OEKO-TEX / CertiPUR-US.** Certifications that the materials have been independently tested. These are real. Look for them.
- **Density (foam).** Measured in pounds per cubic foot. 4+ lb/cu ft is high-density. Anything under 2 lb/cu ft is the foam that breaks down in 18 months.
- **Coil count.** More than 800 individually wrapped coils in a queen is the rough threshold for a quality hybrid.

### Words that mean nothing

- **Premium.** Empty.
- **Cloud-like.** Marketing.
- **Plush comfort.** Adjective, not specification.
- **Cooling technology.** Sometimes real (gel infusion, phase-change material), sometimes a logo. Ask what specifically is doing the cooling.
- **Eco-friendly.** Without certifications, this is a guess.

### Questions to ask any salesperson

1. What is the foam density? (Should be a number.)
2. How many individually wrapped coils, if it's a hybrid? (Should be a number above 800 in a queen.)
3. What certifications? (Should be specific names — GOTS, GOLS, OEKO-TEX, CertiPUR-US.)
4. What's the warranty against impressions, and how deep an impression qualifies? (Should be 10+ years and 1.5 inches or less.)
5. What's the home trial? (Should be 100+ nights, with free returns.)

If a salesperson can't answer any of these without checking, walk.

## Sleep style → mattress fit

Use this section as a starting point. The right mattress is the one that lets you wake up feeling rested. If you're between two options, this is what we'd suggest.

### Side sleepers

You need pressure relief at the shoulders and hips, and enough surface contour to keep your spine straight. Look for a mattress that is **medium-soft to medium-firm** — usually a hybrid with a softer top layer, or a quality memory foam.

In our line: **Busby Slumber** for plush pressure relief, or **Busby Dream** for hybrid feel with motion isolation.

### Back sleepers

You need flat, even support across the lumbar so your hips don't sink. Look for **medium-firm to firm**.

In our line: **Busby Dream** for hybrid feel with strong lumbar support, or **Busby Nod** if you prefer all-foam.

### Stomach sleepers

You need a firm enough surface that your hips don't sink below your shoulders. Look for **medium-firm to firm**, with low surface contour.

In our line: **Busby Nod** or **Busby Doze** — both engineered for even, firm support.

### Combo sleepers (you change positions during the night)

You need a mattress that responds quickly enough that you don't get stuck in a position. Pocket-coil hybrids excel here.

In our line: **Busby Dream**.

### Hot sleepers

Coils breathe. Foam traps heat unless it's specifically engineered with airflow channels. If you sleep hot, prioritize a hybrid or a foam with explicit cooling tech (gel, perforation, phase-change material).

In our line: **Busby Dream** is the most breathable; **Busby Slumber** uses cooling-channel foam.

### Couples with different preferences

Buy a hybrid with strong motion isolation. Pocket coils are non-negotiable here. If your preferences differ by more than one firmness level, look at split-firmness options or split king setups.

In our line: **Busby Dream** is the closest to a "couples mattress" we make. The Slumber's plush feel works for many couples too.
